## Decisions

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Records, scope, and access | Goal (`goal_`), key result (`kr_`), and portfolio (`pf_`) are `core.record` envelopes; check-ins have their own identifier (`gci_`). A goal is workspace-, brand-, or project-scoped and a portfolio is workspace- or brand-scoped. Access is the placement's (workspace or brand level, and the governing project chain), decided at the time of every request. Goals and portfolios are never shareable (not in `SHAREABLE_RECORD_TYPES`); guests and share-only readers have no access (collections are `forbidden`, records `not_found`). OD-06: workspace-level goals are readable by members with workspace-level read. No new authorization action: `record.create`, `record.update`, `record.archive`, `record.restore`, `record.trash`, `record.purge`, and `record.read` are reused. |
| G2 | Periods | A period is a calendar quarter, half, or year, or a custom range of at most ten years (3660 days). Fiscal calendars are out of scope and deferred (DQ-39): they would need a 1C workspace setting. |
| G3 | Progress is computed for the viewer | Nothing derived from linked work is stored. Linked-work progress, health, dependencies, budgets, effort, and orphan findings are computed at read time for the viewer from only the work that viewer may read (OD-07, OD-13). Hidden brands, projects, and tasks change no count and appear in no label, and every aggregate says what it covers (`coverage`, `scope_note`, `as_of`, `computed: live`) and reports incompleteness (`incomplete`, `truncated`, `not_tracked`). The OD-13 formula is done leaf tasks over non-cancelled leaf tasks the viewer may read, in whole percent; a project link covers that project's own tasks (sub-projects are linked separately), and a task link covers the task and its subtasks. |
| G4 | Progress sources | A goal's progress comes from one basis: a manual measurement (baseline, target, current; the direction is the order of baseline and target; percent is clamped to 0 to 100), a manual percentage (no target), the mean of its key results (a plain equal-weight mean of the measured key results, not a 5C metric; it says it is incomplete when some key results are unmeasured), or linked work. Derived progress is never settable by hand. |
| G5 | Updates, confidence, freshness | Check-ins are append-only (`goals.goal_checkin` is immutable by trigger and the application role cannot update or delete it). A goal's and key result's `latest_*` pointers move with the check-in in the same transaction, and a check-in that states no confidence keeps the last stated one. Update freshness is `never`, `fresh`, or `stale` against `GOAL_UPDATE_STALE_DAYS` (default 14), computed from the goal itself on every read and never behind the stored state. Check-ins take an `Idempotency-Key` and a per-member rate limit (`GOAL_CHECKIN_RATE_PER_MINUTE`). |
| G6 | Status and concurrency | Status is draft, active, achieved, missed, or cancelled. A goal that left draft cannot return to it, a closed goal refuses check-ins (`goal_closed`), and an archived goal is read-only (`goal_archived`). Edits carry the row version (`If-Match` or `version`): a stale one is `conflict`, a missing one `precondition_required`. |
| G7 | Links | A goal or one of its key results links projects and tasks. The linker must be able to read the target, and an unreadable, unknown, or wrongly placed target gets the same `invalid_reference`. A brand goal links only its brand's work (a database trigger enforces it too) and a project goal links tasks of its own project. A goal holds at most `GOAL_LINKS_MAX` links. A link grants no access and implies no progress for unreadable work, and the purge of work removes the links that name it. |
| G8 | Portfolios | A portfolio is an explicit list of projects, bounded by `PORTFOLIO_PROJECTS_MAX`; a brand portfolio holds only its brand's projects (trigger). The editor names only projects they can read; members they cannot read are preserved but never listed or counted for them. A workspace holds at most `PORTFOLIOS_MAX` portfolios. |
| G9 | Health rules | Deterministic and explainable (`packages/work/src/goals/semantics.ts#evaluateHealth`). Reasons: `deadline_passed` (off track); `target_passed` (at risk up to 14 days late, off track after); `behind_schedule` (completion lags the elapsed share of time by at least 20 points at risk, 40 off track; only for an active project with a start date, an end date, and tasks); `overdue_tasks` (25% or more of the open tasks at risk, 50% or more off track; active projects); `blocked_tasks` (25% or more of the open tasks, at risk; active projects); `time_budget_exceeded` (off track); and `time_budget_nearly_used` (90% or more used with under 90% of tasks done, at risk). The result is the worst severity. With no evidence the result is `unknown`, never silently on track; a done project is `complete`; a cancelled project is `unknown`. A planned or paused project is judged only by its dates and its time budget, because the task-based rules apply to active projects only. Computed health never overwrites the project's declared health: both are shown, with `differs_from_declared`. `incomplete` lists the missing inputs (no dates, no tasks, no time budget, paused, not started, cancelled). |
| G10 | Dependencies | OD-05 and OD-09: only `blocks` edges between tasks of different projects where both ends are readable. States: `resolved` (the blocker is done or cancelled), `overdue` (open and past its due date), `late` (due after the blocked task), and `open`. An edge to unreadable work is omitted along with every condition derived solely from it, such as a blocked count. |
| G11 | Budgets | Money and AI budgets are shown as set. Spend and AI usage are explicitly not tracked yet (7A usage metering) and reported `not_tracked`. A time budget compares with the readable logged time (3D visibility). Money totals are grouped by currency and never summed across currencies. |
| G12 | Effort | 3D semantics: the remaining estimate, else the estimate less readable logged time (never negative). Unestimated open tasks are counted and reported. |
| G13 | Orphans | Always viewer-relative and labelled so. A project is unlinked when it is planned, active, or paused and has no active readable goal tied to it directly (a link to the project, a link to one of its tasks, or a goal scoped to it) or inherited through a linked ancestor project. A goal is without active work when no readable planned or active project or unfinished task is linked (reasons `no_visible_work` and `no_active_work`). Cancelled and draft goals are not findings. |
| G14 | Background recalculation | `goals.goal_state` stores viewer-neutral facts only (update and period state, the rollup of manual key results, the inputs hash, and the dirty, due, lease, attempt, and error bookkeeping). The `goals.recalculate` job (every minute) discovers due workspaces through `exec_dispatch.discover_goal_work`, claims at most `GOALS_RECALC_BATCH_MAX` goals with a lease (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, `GOALS_RECALC_LEASE_SECONDS`), and recomputes each in its own transaction. A computed check-in (`source = computed`) is appended only when the inputs hash changes (unique per goal and hash, so it is idempotent and overlap-safe). A goal that depends on linked work stores no number (`viewer_dependent`). A failure backs off (2^attempts minutes, capped at `GOALS_RECALC_INTERVAL_MINUTES`) and surfaces as `failing` after `GOALS_RECALC_MAX_ATTEMPTS`; a crashed worker's goals return when the lease lapses; archived goals are skipped with a later look; the goal's state row is the lock, so a concurrent change is never lost. `POST /goals/{id}:recalculate` is the explicit recovery path. |
| G15 | Exports | CSV and JSON of goals (with key results) and of portfolio rows, the viewer's own view only, bounded by `GOALS_EXPORT_ROWS_MAX` (a larger export is refused, not truncated), audited (`goals.exported`, `portfolio.exported`, and a security event of category export), with formula cells neutralized. Nothing is stored. |
| G16 | Departure | The `goal_owner` ownership hook moves a departing member's goals, key results, and portfolios to the member chosen in the departure plan. Check-ins stay under the author's name (shown as "Former member" afterwards). |
| G17 | Lifecycle | Adapters for `goal` (it carries its key results), `key_result`, and `portfolio`. A project's lifecycle carries the goals scoped to it. Purge of a project or task removes the goal links and portfolio memberships that name it (`purgeGoalTargets`), and purge of a goal deletes its key results, links, check-ins, and state. |
| G18 | Deliberately not in 5B | Comments, mentions, and following on goals (4A host registration; it would need a collaboration migration); notifications for stale updates (the event `goals.goal.update_stale` is emitted for 4B to consume later); goals in search (5A); dashboards and reports and the brand time and AI investment rollups (5D, GOAL-011); metric definitions and weighted rollups (5C; OD-13 allows no estimate weighting until 5C, DQ-40); money spend and AI usage tracking (7A); fiscal calendars (DQ-39); goal hierarchies and alignment; templates; automatic status changes; and capacity-versus-effort comparison (3E data is not used by 5B: effort is reported from estimates and logged time only). |
| G19 | Observability | Signals `goals.reads` (view, outcome), `goals.changes` (kind, outcome), and `goals.jobs` (kind, outcome). No goal text, note, title, or identifier appears in metrics, logs, or events (events carry enums and counts). Audit stores content hashes for titles, descriptions, and notes. |

## Migration and rollback evidence

Migration `0041_goals_portfolios` creates the `goals` schema, the `goals.valid_value` function, seven tables (`goal`, `key_result`, `goal_checkin`, `goal_link`, `goal_state`, `portfolio`, `portfolio_project`), the check-in immutability trigger, the link and portfolio-member integrity triggers, the scheduler's column grants and discovery policy, and `exec_dispatch.discover_goal_work`. Its down migration (`0041_goals_portfolios.down.sql`) drops everything the up migration created, in key order, and restores the exact 0040 catalog. The numbered migrations 0001 to 0040 are untouched and none is reused.

Executable evidence, in `packages/db/test/integration/migrations.test.ts`:
- "upgrades through every accepted boundary (0009–0041) to the candidate and reverses each boundary step": for each accepted boundary it fingerprints objects, constraints, grants, and policies, steps up, steps down to the previous boundary and compares the exact catalog, steps up again and compares, then reaches the one-shot candidate catalog. The 0041 boundary is the last step (`down` to 40 equals the 0040 catalog and `up` to 41 equals the 0041 catalog).
- "reverses completely with down migrations and re-applies cleanly": every down migration runs to version 0 and the full catalog fingerprint after re-applying equals the first.

Related catalog tests: `packages/db/test/integration/workspace-integrity.test.ts` (the seven `goals.*` tables carry the database-enforced workspace key, and the 37 foreign keys of the 5B tables are counted) and `packages/platform/test/integration/rls.test.ts` (all seven tables have `tenant_isolation` RLS and `discover_goal_work` is one of the 18 discovery functions).

## Consequences

The API gains the goal and portfolio routes (PL-38) and the worker gains the `goals.recalculate` job (every minute); 13 named parameters (owner 5B), 11 events, 3 telemetry signals, the identifier prefix `gci`, and four non-resource envelopes (`portfolio_overview`, `portfolio_dependencies`, `portfolio_orphans`, and `goal_work`) are registered. No action, role, or setting of 1C is added. Because nothing derived from linked work is stored, a portfolio read costs a bounded evaluation (`PORTFOLIO_READ_PROJECTS_MAX`) on every request, and it says when it truncated; if that cost matters, a later phase may add viewer-keyed caching with explicit invalidation, which this ADR does not do. Phase 5D builds dashboards and reports on these reads, 5C may replace the key-result mean and the completion basis, 7A supplies spend and AI usage, and 4B may consume `goals.goal.update_stale`.
