## Decisions

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Following (OD-10) | A follower is one explicit, per-record subscription (`relations.follower`, source `explicit`). Only the person themselves follows or unfollows, through `PUT` and `DELETE` on the record's follow resource, which are immediate and idempotent and need `record.follow` (view) at the time. Following is never inherited from workspace, brand, or project membership, assignment, ownership, or a received share, and it grants nothing. A follower who can no longer read the record is inert: omitted from the Following view, follower lists, and counts, never reported to anyone else, and removed when the member departs or the record is purged. Someone else's follow rows are never listed to a viewer who cannot read the record through ownership. A share-only reader sees only their own state. |
| D2 | Comment edit, delete, and audit (OD-38) | An author may edit or delete their own comment only within `COMMENT_EDIT_WINDOW_MINUTES` of posting. Every edit appends the prior body to `collab.comment_revision` (append-only; no application update or delete). Deletion is a tombstone: the body leaves the row, the prior body joins the trail, and the thread keeps its shape. Nobody edits someone else's comment, administrators included. A person with `comment.moderate` (manage on the record) may delete, never edit, another person's comment at any time, and the tombstone says a moderator removed it. Revisions are readable by the author and by people with moderation authority on the record. Audit events carry content hashes, never bodies. On purge, bodies, revisions, reactions, and mentions are removed and the structural audit facts remain. |
| D3 | Threads | Threads are flat: a root comment and replies that name the root and the comment they answer, so replies never nest deeper. A reply to a deleted comment is allowed (the tombstone stays in place). Lists page root comments by cursor with at most `COMMENT_REPLIES_INLINE_MAX` replies inline and the reply count; further replies are a cursor read. A record holds at most `COMMENTS_PER_RECORD_MAX` comments, a body at most `COMMENT_BODY_MAX_CHARS`, in the rich-text v1 encoding. Commenting needs `record.comment` (comment level), so read-only members cannot, share-only guests with a comment share can, and an archived record needs the workspace's `comment_on_archived` policy. |
| D4 | Mentions | A comment names its mentions explicitly as public identifiers. The server resolves only people, teams, and records the author may name in that record's scope: an active member who can read the host record, a team, or a task or project the author can read. Hidden, unknown, ineligible, and agent targets (no agents exist before 8B) all return one `not_mentionable` issue. Guests may not mention people or teams. A mention grants nothing, never notifies in 4A (`notified` stays false for 4B), and is shown only while its target is still readable by the viewer. Each member or team mention emits `relations.mention.created`. |
| D5 | Reactions | A fixed vocabulary of eight reactions. A person sets or clears their own reaction on a readable comment (`PUT` and `DELETE`, idempotent). Lists carry counts and the viewer's own reactions, never who else reacted. |
| D6 | Activity and disclosure | Comments, reactions, mentions, reviews, delegation, and reassignment are authorized through their host record on every read; nothing is projected or cached with access baked in. Audit events and the 3B activity view show that something happened and who did it, never a comment body, and activity drops any change that names a record, person, field, or prior value the viewer cannot read. A stranger gets the same `not_found` for an unknown and an unreadable comment, record, or review. |
| D7 | Reviews | A review request targets one record at one version (`subject_version`) with up to `REVIEW_REVIEWERS_MAX` named reviewers (active internal members who can read the subject), a rule (`any` or `all`), and a kind (`review` or `approval`). Requesting needs `review.request` through ownership (a share does not). A decision needs `review.decide`, a named reviewer who is still eligible, and the request's current version; it is immutable and idempotent. Outcomes are typed: `approved`, `changes_requested`, `rejected`. Under `any` the first decision settles; under `all` every reviewer must approve and any other outcome settles at once. The subject changing after the request makes decisions fail with `subject_changed`; the requester refreshes (supersedes) the request. Deciding one's own request needs `review.self_approve` and the workspace `self_approval_mode` (OD-20), and is labelled. At most one request is open per record. |
| D8 | Delegation and reassignment | Delegating moves the delegator's own assignment to another eligible member (active, able to read the task), records `delegated_from` on the assignment, and keeps the primary flag. Reassigning through the assignee set records who was added and removed. Both append to `collab.assignment_event` (never updated), and a departing member's assignments are recorded as `unassigned` by departure. The history is readable only through ownership (a share-only reader gets `not_found`), and each person it names is shown only to viewers who may see people. Neither command changes access. |
| D9 | Concurrency and idempotency | Every mutation takes an `Idempotency-Key`. Edits and decisions use `If-Match` versions. A review request is locked while it is decided, so concurrent deciders get one settlement and one `conflict`. Comment creation is rate limited per person (`COMMENT_RATE_PER_MINUTE`). |
| D10 | Contracts and boundaries | Events are `collab.comment.created`, `.edited`, `.deleted`, `.reaction_changed`; `relations.mention.created`; `relations.follow.added` and `.removed`; `collab.review.requested`, `.decided`, `.withdrawn`, `.superseded`; and `work.task.delegated`. Payloads carry identifiers and flags only. 4A emits canonical events and creates no notification, preference, or delivery (4B), document comments or suggestions (4C), collaborative editing or presence (4D), file attachments (4E), or agent review automation (8D); reviewers and authors are people. Subject kinds are tasks and projects until their owning phases register more. |

## Recorded deviations and clarifications

- **Review rule and decisions.** 0B described one terminal decision per request. With the `all` rule several reviewers decide, so the immutable record is one decision per named reviewer, and the request settles at most once. The model entry is updated.
- **Mentions are structured.** The rich-text envelope (v1) has no mention node before 4C, so mentions travel beside the body as resolved identifiers; the visible `@name` text is only text.
- **Related 2C approvals.** Workflow transition approvals (`workflow.approval_request`) stay the gate for status transitions. Review requests are the general review and approval flow for tasks and projects, and they do not change status.
- **Authorship is content.** Readers of a comment see its author's display name (never an email), because authorship is part of the comment they are allowed to read.

## Consequences

Migration `0036_collaboration_reviews` reverses exactly to 0035. Task and project pages hold comments, following, reviews, and delegation; My Work gains working Following and Reviews tabs; the 3B activity view shows comment, review, and assignment entries without bodies.
